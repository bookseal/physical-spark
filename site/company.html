Physical Spark is the data and talent engine for Physical AI. Developers solve gamified robot missions, peer-review each other's runs on video, and climb a board. Two assets accumulate automatically — verified robot demonstration data, and verified records of who can actually do the work. We sell both to robotics companies. The missions are how they arrive.

Why now

The hard part stopped being the model. It is the data, and then it is the people.

The data bottleneck

Robot bodies got cheap and robot brains got funded. What nobody has enough of is real manipulation data — many hands, many rooms, many failures. Labs generate it in-house, which is expensive and narrow: one building, one set of habits, one definition of "done".

A leaderboard generates it as a by-product of people wanting to win. And the step that usually costs the most and therefore gets skipped — labelling quality — is already happening, because peer review is the label.

Then the bottleneck moves

As robot foundation models keep getting better at the general case, the scarce thing stops being the policy and starts being the person who can put one in front of a real machine, in a real building, and make it hold.

We are not putting a date on that. We are saying that when it happens, the useful thing to own is a ranked list of people whose work is already on video, dated and reviewed — and that list has to be built before the demand shows up, not after.

Business model

Three layers, one loop.

  1. 1 Mission seasonsearly revenue Paid, time-boxed seasons with a completion deposit. Cash from day one — and the acquisition channel for everything above it. This is the layer that makes the data arrive.
  2. 2 Datasets & benchmarks for labsthe core business Verified demonstrations in LeRobot format with the peer review attached as a quality label. Plus sponsored seasons: a lab posts the task it actually cares about and buys the spread — what works, what breaks, how often — instead of one polished demo.
  3. 3 Verified talent pipelineexpansion The board as a ranked, video-backed supply of Physical AI engineers. Introductions first, placement later. A skill proven on camera outranks a line on a CV, and it comes with a date.

Why this is one business, not three

Layer 1 is how the data arrives. Layer 3 is what the data proves about the people who made it. Layer 2 is the thing we actually sell.

The flywheel is that each season makes the next dataset and the next group of ranked builders both larger and cheaper to produce. Missions are the acquisition channel; they are not the product.

Season pricing is published on the market page as first-season numbers, not a rate card. Layers 2 and 3 are not priced here because those products do not exist yet.

Roadmap

Written as states, not dates.

  • Now The loop runs end to end in simulation. Missions are live, the board is live, and anyone with a laptop can complete a run without owning a robot.
  • Next Run upload and the peer-review gallery. Every verified run lands in the dataset in LeRobot format, with its review attached.
  • Then Season one, run in the open. The numbers we report are completion rate and review throughput — the two that say whether the engine actually turns.
  • After that The first lab-posted benchmark season. An outside team defines the task; the board attacks it. That is the first time layer 2 is a product and not a plan.
  • The direction The board is where a robotics company looks first — for the demonstrations and for the person.
