## 0. The thesis — commoditization is an argument *for* this school

Generating software stopped being the hard part. Describe an app, it appears. So the price of "I
can use the tool" collapsed and value moved up the stack: deciding **what** to build and **why**,
designing the system, evaluating it, keeping it alive.

The usual conclusion is "so teach people to think, not to code." Ours is narrower and more
defensible: **physical AI is where that commoditization has not arrived.** A robot arm either
picks up the block or it does not. No prompt gets you past a gripper that misses by two
centimetres.

That is also why the grading model works here and not in a general AI course. When output is a
video of a physical outcome, peer review has something objective to grade. This is the same wedge
[hate-map.md](hate-map.md) found from the other direction: the loudest complaint about existing
robotics courses is that you can *"audit for free without graded feedback,"* and learners who go
it alone stall. Free courses are not the gap. **Graded feedback is.**

## 1. Three countries, three engines

The first question is never "what do we teach." It is **who writes the cheque** — and the answer
differs so much that the same curriculum needs a different lever in each market.

| | 🇰🇷 Korea | 🇺🇸 United States | 🇸🇬 Singapore |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who pays** | The state, then the individual | The employer, then the individual | The state, via an approved institution |
| **Mechanism** | 국민내일배움카드 → K-Digital Training (KDT) | Corporate L&D budgets; marketplaces (Udemy, Coursera) and cohort platforms (Maven) for individuals | SkillsFuture Credit against *qualifying* programmes only |
| **The lever** | **Get approved** — distribution is state-controlled | **Differentiate** — supply is unlimited, acquisition is expensive | **Partner** — you cannot simply list a course (see §2) |
| **The catch** | Price anchored near zero; students expect state-funded to mean free | Marketplaces rent you trust, then keep pricing power and the customer list | Small market, but English-speaking and the ASEAN gateway |

### Korea — better than the standard summary suggests

- **AI Campus** launches inside KDT in **H1 2026**, targeting **10,000 AI specialists** at **zero
  self-pay**, while general KDT programmes begin charging **up to 10% self-pay** from 2026-01-01.
- KDT training allowance rises to **₩200,000/month** (from ₩116,000). 내일배움카드 base is
  **₩3M over 5 years**, up to **₩5M**.
- **The consequential detail:** whether Physical Spark is classified as an *AI* track or a
  *robotics/hardware* track decides whether students pay 0% or 10%. That classification is worth
  more than any pricing decision we could make ourselves.

### United States — the enterprise budget is the buyer

- Enterprise AI spending **$407B in 2026**, up **34.8%** from $302B. **86%** of enterprises are
  raising AI budgets; roughly **2%** are cutting.
- **75%** of organisations are increasing AI-related L&D spend; **25%** name AI training a top
  funding priority for 2026.
- Average senior-leader L&D budget **$11.8M**; per-employee spend ranges from **over $1,700** at
  small organisations to **under $300** at large ones.
- Implication: rung 5 of the ladder (B2B workshops) aims at a budget line that is growing, while
  rungs 1–2 fight for individual attention in the most crowded course market on earth.

## 2. Where the standard advice is wrong — Singapore

The advice you will hear, and the version in most market summaries, is: *"register with
SkillsFuture and absorb the subsidised demand."* **It does not survive the eligibility rules.**

- **Short courses, online subscriptions and standalone workshops generally do NOT qualify** for
  the **S$4,000** SkillsFuture Credit (Mid-Career).
- What does qualify is narrower: full qualifications from an Institute of Higher Learning,
  shorter modules that **stack toward** a full qualification, MOE-subsidised arts qualifications,
  SkillsFuture Career Transition Programmes (train-and-place), and courses tied to Progressive
  Wage Model sectors.
- Citizens aged 40+ hold up to **S$4,500** combined (S$500 base + S$4,000 mid-career). The 2020
  **S$500 tranche expired 2025-12-31** and cannot be applied to courses starting on or after
  2026-01-01.

**Consequence for us:** a six-week online cohort — exactly what Physical Spark is designed to run
— would **not** be credit-eligible on its own. Singapore is only reachable by partnering with a
polytechnic or university so the missions become stackable modules inside an already-approved
qualification. That is slower and relationship-shaped, so it sequences **third**, not first.

We publish the correction rather than the pitch-friendly version for the same reason the
[Korea playbook](../07-strategy/physical-ai-korea-playbook.md) does: a plan built on a rule that
does not exist fails later, and more expensively.

## 3. The revenue ladder

Rungs, not options — each is built on the audience the one below produced. Individuals supply
volume; companies supply price.

1. **Self-paced missions** — cheap, infinite capacity, no completion pressure. Top of funnel, not
   the business.
2. **Cohort (live, fixed dates)** — higher price, real completion rates, real testimonials. Where
   peer review actually happens, and where the 빛해빗 deposit-and-forfeit mechanic ports in.
3. **Membership / community** — turns one cohort into recurring revenue, keeps the gallery alive
   between intakes.
4. **Kits, templates, licences** — arm bundles and mission packs. Margin without teaching hours.
5. **B2B workshops / internal training** — highest price per sale, aimed directly at the AI
   reskilling budget that 75% of organisations are growing.

Skipping to rung 5 without rungs 1–2 means selling enterprise training with no evidence it works.

## 4. Channel — borrow trust first, own the list second

A marketplace (Udemy, Coursera) lends traffic and credibility on day one, and takes pricing power,
the customer list and a cut. Your own site (Teachable, Kajabi, Circle) gives you price, list,
brand and data, and hands you the traffic problem. The sequence is the point: a marketplace is a
**loan** of trust, and loans get repaid. Move the cohort in-house once the review gallery has runs
worth watching.

## 5. What blocks a launch before content does

- **Payments** — multi-currency pricing, local payment methods (they move conversion more than
  price does), cross-border refunds.
- **Tax** — US residents are taxed on worldwide income; Korean-sourced online course income is
  reportable; EU VAT on digital goods; withholding varies by country.
- **Status** — depending on visa type, teaching online can count as work. Settle this with a
  professional **before** relocating or launching.

None of it is interesting and all of it stops a launch harder than a weak curriculum.
