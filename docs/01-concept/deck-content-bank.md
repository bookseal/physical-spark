## 3 · Developers  *(35s)*

**Headline** — Developers cannot / get hired.

**Sub** — The work did not disappear. It moved to a rung most of them are not on.

- **Seniors too**
- **75** — US software job ads today · Feb 2020 = 100
- **159** — machine learning job ads · same scale
- **back → full stack → product**

**Script** (85w) — Start with what is happening to developers. Picture one — ten years in, good at his job, not getting hired. Here is the clearest picture. Index US job ads to February 2020. Software peaked at two hundred and thirty in 2022, and today it sits at seventy-five. Machine learning, same scale, sits at one hundred and fifty-nine. So the work did not vanish. It moved one rung up — backend, frontend, full stack, product engineer. He is standing on a rung that is emptying out.

## 4 · Companies  *(35s)*

**Headline** — One job is hiring / like crazy.

**Sub** — The forward deployed engineer — the person who puts AI inside a company.

- **5% → 70%** — of companies hiring an FDE, in two quarters
- **5% → 40%** — of company apps carry an AI agent by year end
- **#1** — AI is the hardest skill to hire, worldwide
- **72%** — of employers cannot fill roles

**Script** (71w) — And yet one job is hiring like mad. At the start of this year, five to ten percent of companies planned to hire a forward deployed engineer. By the end of the second quarter it was seventy. And ManpowerGroup asked thirty-nine thousand employers across forty-one countries what is hardest to hire. For the first time on record, the answer was AI skills. So the jobs exist. They just cannot be filled.

## 5 · How they hire now  *(35s)*

**Headline** — The coding test is dead. / Now it is take-homes.

**Sub** — Every filter they had measures something that no longer sorts people.

- **17,000 / 2,000** — US engineers who claim it / who can do it
- **Coding test**
- **No veterans**

**Script** (80w) — Because nobody can tell who is real. One study counted seventeen thousand forward deployed engineers on the US market, and about two thousand who can actually deliver. Our man is somewhere in that pile and there is no way to find him. The coding test cannot separate them — it measured exactly the part AI now does for you. And nobody has ten years in a role that is two years old. So what is left is take-homes and hackathons.

**Image** — `assets/brand/deck-2-nobody-can-tell.svg`

## 6 · Robots are next  *(40s)*

**Headline** — Next year it hits robots. / And there every filter breaks.

**Sub** — The body got cheap. Now every factory floor needs somebody who can drive it.

- **VLA**
- **1,220** — robots per 10,000 workers · Korea is first
- **Every plant**

**Script** (100w) — Now look at robots. Five years ago you could say "move that box" and nothing happened. Vision-language-action models changed that — you say it in plain words and the arm moves. An arm is two hundred dollars now. So next year those companies want that engineer plus a factory floor. And this is not a few research labs. It is every plant with a line that stops and nobody on site who can retrain the arm. Every filter breaks at once — no bench, no veterans, no recruiter. And a shift like this does not lift everyone. It sorts them.

**Image** — `assets/brand/deck-14-filters-fail.svg`

## 7 · Why they cannot cross  *(35s)*

**Headline** — With software you can practise. / With robots you cannot.

**Sub** — And nobody senior says out loud that they do not know where to start.

- **Software**
- **Robots**
- **No feedback**

**Script** (83w) — Robotics has a second problem software does not. To practise AI you need an API key and a laptop. To practise robotics you need hardware, space, and somebody to say what to learn first. And here is the part nobody writes down: a developer with ten years behind them will not stand up and say they do not know where to start. So they do not cross. It is not nerve — there is no way in that does not cost them face.
